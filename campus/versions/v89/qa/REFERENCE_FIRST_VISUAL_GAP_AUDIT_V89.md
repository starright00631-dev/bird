## Why this audit exists

The v89 inventory pass resolved component names and counted meshes, but that does not demonstrate that the buildings look like the reference data. This pass compares several exact/strongly identified photographs against the current building-specific source geometry. The first comparisons confirm that some of the most important facades are still represented mainly as plain envelopes.

The rendered views used for geometry diagnosis are source-mesh-only diagnostic renders. They intentionally do not claim to reproduce the full browser viewer's PBR/material appearance. T2/T3 have embedded tile textures, so their material appearance must be checked separately in the actual viewer.

## Important nuance: not every building is equally under-modeled

- **T2 and T3** already have more substantial geometry and embedded 384×384 base-color tile textures on their main facade meshes. T2 also has six portico columns and several photo-supported glazing/frame groups; T3 has six portico columns and separate ribbon-glazing groups. The tile textures are brick/tile appearance, not a complete facade photograph. Their official photos should be compared with the actual browser viewer before declaring the facade complete or missing.
- **T4** has four continuous ribbon-glazing strips, opaque returns, and stair components. Its photo-to-model comparison is still needed.
- **T5–T8** have story-edge strips and some stair components, but no separately identified glazing nodes in the current building inventory. This is a high-priority evidence check, not yet a fully proven real-world omission.
- **Phase-I LRC** has 353 registered components / 22,132 faces, including many glazing and stair nodes. Its distinctive projecting frame, diagonal braces, pillars and skylight must be checked from low front/side views; a roof-biased isometric view is insufficient to judge it.

## Reconstruction method from here

1. **T1 first:** acquire at least two additional independent T1 views (front/side or campus photo archive). Establish a stable camera/scale relationship using known facade planes and the existing source mesh. Then build a reversible facade candidate for the observed ribbon glazing, deep white frames, lower glazing and canopy. Do not infer hidden elevations from the single current photo.
2. **V29 next:** seek an official V29 view and a second angle. Reconstruct the observed balcony/window rhythm and cladding zones only after the image can be assigned to the correct building side and aligned to the model.
3. **D1 next:** obtain D1-A and D1-B views, including the opposite side and roof. Verify the official D1 section split and compare it against the current single primary mass.
4. **V25:** treat the 62-vertex primary mesh as a geometry-resolution blocker; acquire a V25-specific photo set before reconstructing.
5. **Phase-II LRC/Lab:** map the distinct asset separately using authoritative location evidence and multiple as-built views; keep the project rendering as design evidence, not surveyed geometry.
6. Repeat the same reference → camera/identity → model comparison → reversible candidate → independent validation cycle for every remaining building.

## Acceptance gate

A building is not considered visually reconstructed merely because its selector entry or source anchor exists. It needs:
- a building-specific reference set with identity confidence;
- front/side/rear/roof/plan comparison where available;
- explicit missing-vs-present feature analysis at mesh/material/viewer level;
- reversible candidate geometry or a documented reason why no edit is yet justified;
- independent before/after review against at least one view not used to construct the candidate;
- unchanged source hashes for untouched assets and a reproducible package/CI pass.

No speculative facade, window, balcony, roof or entrance geometry is promoted to the physical master in this audit.
