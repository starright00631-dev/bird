# Intervention evidence matrix

This matrix distinguishes mechanism plausibility, published evidence and what can actually be recommended at BNBU. No row provides a BNBU-specific percentage reduction.

| Option | Mechanism addressed | Evidence and design relevance | BNBU decision gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-pane exterior visible markers | Makes transparent/reflective glass visible as an obstacle | Environment and Climate Change Canada guidance describes full-pane exterior patterns with no gaps over 5 cm and elements at least 5 mm. ABC's 2025 prescriptive rating distinguishes 2x2 and 2x4 patterns and considers more than spacing alone. Ottawa 2026 and Oklahoma 2026 provide field evidence with different designs and uncertainty. | First retrofit candidate for verified untreated hazardous glass. Measure full-pane coverage, spacing, marker geometry, surface, contrast and appearance under local light. |
| External cords or screens | Physically interrupts the approach to glass | Klem et al. 2013 and field practice provide candidate evidence, but efficacy and transfer depend on configuration. | Alternative only after checking mesh/cord spacing, stand-off, entanglement, wind load, fire/egress, maintenance and transfer of contact to the screen or frame. |
| External shading / solar screen | Reduces a measured reflected habitat image | Mechanistically plausible where a pane-specific reflection is demonstrated; not a universal collision treatment effect. | Test only after multi-time, multi-angle approach-view measurements establish the reflection pathway. Check daylight, structural loading and maintenance. |
| Seasonal night-light controls | Reduces artificial-light attraction/disorientation during nocturnal migration | Van Doren et al. 2021 provides a long-term single-building observational basis; regional and seasonal transfer remains uncertain. | Require local nocturnal migrant activity and measured artificial light. Keep separate from daytime transparency/reflection actions. |
| Vegetation reconfiguration | Changes bird attraction, approach routing, obstruction or reflected scene | China 2025 and Ottawa 2026 show scale- and context-dependent associations. These do not establish a universal tree-removal effect. | Never remove trees from canopy percentage alone. Require route, resource, reflection and ecological-impact evidence. Treat glass first when vegetation is only the reflected scene. |
| Interior blinds / indoor plant changes | Reduces some through-glass false-passage cues | Supplementary mechanism; does not necessarily remove exterior mirror reflection. | Use only as a supplement after identifying a transmitted-scene pathway; do not substitute for exterior treatment on reflective glass. |
| UV-only pattern | Provides a potentially visible cue to species with suitable visual sensitivity | Swaddle et al. 2020 tested two songbird species in controlled tunnel conditions; results are not universal across species, weather or low light. | Defer as a primary treatment until full-assembly spectral measurements and local species/illumination tests are available. |

## Evidence interpretation

- **ECCC guidance** is an installation screen, not a BNBU trial.
- **ABC 2025** is a prescriptive rating procedure; spacing alone does not confer a Threat Factor rating or guarantee a field effect.
- **Lysyk et al. 2026 (Ottawa)** reports 225 collisions involving 51 species and compares treatment compliance. Its coefficients are study-specific.
- **Riding et al. 2026 (Oklahoma)** uses a before-after-control-impact design with six treated façades; the formal treatment-by-period interaction was not statistically significant. This is useful as a design precedent, not proof of a universal percentage.
- **China 2025** covers 3,078 buildings and motivates multi-scale vegetation/ALAN features, but its observational associations are not intervention effects.
- **Swaddle et al. 2020** supports a species- and condition-specific UV hypothesis, not UV-only treatment for every local species.

## Ranking rule

The current decision engine compares mechanism fit and required evidence. It deliberately does not rank interventions by efficacy or emit a best option until comparable approach denominators, independently verified glass/opaque contacts, matched or randomized intervention monitoring, detection/persistence correction, cost, maintenance, safety and ecological side effects are available.
